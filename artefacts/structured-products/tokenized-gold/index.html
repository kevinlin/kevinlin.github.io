Tokenized Gold

A gold token is an entry on a digital ledger that stands for a weight of gold in a vault. Its price follows gold one for one. It has no strike, barrier or coupon, so the questions to ask are about the wrapper: who holds the metal, what the holder can claim, and what it costs to get in and out.

Gold in a vault, a record on a ledger

An issuer buys gold bars and stores them. It then creates tokens on a ledger, each one standing for a set weight of that gold, and sells them. The ledger records who holds how many. When a token changes hands, the claim on the gold moves with it and the bars stay where they are.

This is an old arrangement on new record-keeping. A gold savings account and a share in a gold fund do the same job. Tokens change three things: the unit can be very small, a transfer settles in minutes, and with some tokens the holder can move the record without going through the issuer.

Tokens come in two kinds, and they differ more than the shared name suggests. A bank token lives on the bank's own ledger. It can be bought, sold and transferred only inside that bank, at the bank's price. A public-chain token lives on a network such as Ethereum. Anyone with a wallet can hold it, and it trades on crypto exchanges at whatever price buyers and sellers agree.

On this set of pages it is the one product with no option inside. A fall of 10% in gold is a fall of 10% in the token. The yield is zero.

Question 1Whose gold is it?Ownership of metal, or a promise from the issuer. And whether specific bars are set aside for holders.
Question 2How do I get out?Sell to the issuer, sell on a market, or take the metal. Each token allows a different mix.
Question 3What does it cost?A margin on each trade, a yearly fee, or both. Which is cheaper depends on how long the gold is held.

Who holds what

Between the holder and the bar there are always the same three things: an account or wallet, a ledger, and an issuer with a vault. The two kinds of token fill them differently.

Allocated and unallocated. Allocated gold is specific bars set aside for the holders, identified by serial number. Unallocated gold is a balance the provider owes, with no bar behind any one customer. Paxos and Tether both describe their backing as allocated and let a holder look up the bars linked to a wallet address. This definition is standard market usage and is not quoted from the sources.

What can go wrong

From HSBC's risk warning and Tether Gold's terms and risk disclosure.

Gold itselfNo protectionThe token falls when gold falls. HSBC states that the holder can lose the entire investment in the worst case.
One buyerThe issuer sets the exitAn HSBC token can be sold only to HSBC, at its price, and HSBC may suspend dealing. Tether states it has no obligation to buy back tokens or bars.
Price gapToken and gold can differOn an exchange a token may trade above or below an ounce of gold. Only someone able to redeem whole bars can close the gap.
Off-hoursConvenience costsHSBC's margin cap rises from 2% to 5% outside gold trading hours in London and Hong Kong.
The chainKeys and forksA public-chain token is controlled by a wallet key. Tether's terms also warn that a blockchain can split into two versions.
Not a depositNot a time depositHSBC states the token is not a time deposit and is not equivalent to one.

Terms

TermMeaning
Troy ounceThe unit for weighing precious metals, written toz. About 31.1 grams.
London Good Delivery barThe bar that meets the London Bullion Market Association's standard. It weighs roughly 400 troy ounces, and no two weigh the same.
LBMALondon Bullion Market Association, which sets the standard for bars and vaults.
TokenA unit recorded on a digital ledger. Here, a claim on a set weight of gold.
Distributed ledgerA record kept in step across several computers. A bank can run a private one. A public blockchain is one that anyone can read and use.
Fractional ownershipOwning a share of a bar and not a whole bar.
RedemptionGiving tokens back to the issuer for metal or for money.
Bank marginThe amount HSBC adds to its selling price and takes off its buying price. It is the only charge on the HSBC token.
AttestationAn accountant's report that the gold held matches the tokens issued. Paxos publishes one each month.
WalletSoftware or hardware that holds the key controlling tokens on a public chain.

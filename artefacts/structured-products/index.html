Structured Products

Four notes that private banks in Asia sell on single shares and baskets: ELN, KOELN, FCN and BEN. In every one of them the investor sells a put option on a share. They differ in how the investor is paid for it and in what can end the note early.

The four notes, in reading order

Each one adds a feature to the one before it, so the order matters the first time through. The small chart on each card is profit or loss at maturity against the final price of the share.

Step 1Yield enhancement
ELN

Equity-Linked Note

Bought below face value. Repays face value if the share closes at or above the strike on the last day. Otherwise the investor gets the share at the strike price.

The base caseA deposit plus a sold put. The discount is the whole income.

Step 2Yield enhancement
KOELN

Knock-Out ELN

An ELN that is redeemed early at face value as soon as the share closes at or above a knock-out price.

Adds to the ELNA knock-out level and daily observation. Same discount, earned sooner if the share rallies.

Step 3Yield enhancement
FCN

Fixed Coupon Note

Bought at face value. Pays a fixed coupon every month while it is alive, usually on the worst share of a basket.

Adds to the KOELNCoupons in place of the discount, a basket, and a knock-in barrier that keeps the put switched off until it is breached.

Open the page →
Step 4Participation
BEN

Bonus Enhanced Note

Bought at face value, with no coupon. At maturity it pays a bonus, or the rise of the worst share if that is higher. Below the strike the bonus is lost.

Swaps inOne large payment on the last day and an uncapped upside, in place of the FCN's monthly coupon and knock-out.

All four carry the same two risks that no level removes. The issuer can fail, and the investor is then an unsecured creditor. And selling the note before maturity can return far less than was paid.

Equity-Linked Note

An ELN is bought below its face value and repays the face value if a share holds above a strike price. If the share ends below the strike, the investor gets the share. A knock-out ELN adds one rule: the note ends early, at face value, as soon as the share rallies to a set price.

A deposit and a sold put in one note

The investor pays a price below 100% of the notional, for example 97%. The note pays no coupon. On the final valuation date one closing price decides the result. If the share closes at or above the strike price, the note repays 100% in cash and the investor keeps the 3% difference. If the share closes below the strike price, the investor receives shares instead of cash: the notional divided by the strike price.

That is what a person gets who places a deposit and, at the same time, sells a put option on the share. The discount is the deposit interest plus the option premium. The premium is the larger part, which is why the yield is far above a deposit rate.

The trade is a view that the share will stay flat or rise a little. A strong rally earns the investor nothing extra, because the gain stops at the discount. A fall is carried in full below the strike price.

Issuers differ on the edge case. UBS and uSMART pay par when the share closes at or above the strike price. BOCI's example pays par only when it closes above. This page uses at or above. Read the term sheet for the note in front of you.

What the knock-out adds

A KOELN is the same note with one more level, set above the initial price. UBS calls it the callable price. If the share reaches it on an observation day, the note is redeemed early at 100%.

The investor earns the whole discount in less time, so the yearly rate of the finished trade is higher. The amount of money is the same. The cost is that the cash comes back early, into a market that has just risen, and the investor has to find something else to do with it.

Before the knock-out, nothing is different from a plain ELN. If the share never reaches the knock-out price, the strike price decides at maturity as usual. The knock-out does not protect against a fall.

How the knock-out is observed changes the odds. A daily-close knock-out ignores a price that spikes during the day and falls back. A continuous knock-out, which BOCI describes, looks at every traded price and so ends the note more easily. This page models the daily close.

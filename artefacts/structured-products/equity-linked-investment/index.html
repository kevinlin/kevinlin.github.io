Equity-Linked Investment

An ELI is the version of an equity-linked note that may be sold to the public in Hong Kong. The payoff is the one you already know from the fixed coupon note. What changes is the vocabulary, the documents and the steps a bank must take before and after the sale.

Same engine, different wrapper

The investor in an ELI sells a put option on a share to the issuer and is paid for it. If the share finishes at or above the strike price, the investor gets the nominal amount back in cash. If it finishes below, the investor has to buy the share at the strike price. A basket ELI applies this to the worst-performing share. An early call ends the investment when the shares do well, and an airbag keeps the put switched off until a lower level is reached. All of this is the mechanism of the fixed coupon note.

The name ELI is used in Hong Kong for the form of this product that the Securities and Futures Commission has authorised for sale to the public. It is unlisted, and it falls under the SFC's Code on Unlisted Structured Investment Products. That code adds duties that a privately placed note does not have: standard offering documents, a cooling-off right on longer products and a commitment by the issuer to buy the product back at regular intervals.

For the placement flow this means two things. The trade economics can reuse the FCN model. The order process needs extra states, because a retail order can wait, be cancelled after it is placed, and be sold back on fixed days.

How the income is paid

This is the main difference in the economics. An FCN always pays a fixed coupon. An ELI can pay in four ways, and the placement flow needs a field for which one applies.

StyleHow it worksWorked exampleExtra fields
DiscountThe investor pays less than the nominal amount and receives the nominal amount at maturity.Pay 97,799 for a nominal 100,000. Maximum gain 2,201.Purchase price
Fixed distributionThe investor pays the nominal amount and receives a fixed amount on each distribution date. This is the FCN.20,000 × 1.5% a quarter = 300 each quarter.Coupon rate, distribution dates
Conditional distributionThe amount for a period is paid only if the share closes at or above a coupon accrual price on that period's observation date.300 for the quarter if the share closes at or above 46.00 on the observation date. Otherwise nothing.Coupon accrual price, distribution observation dates
Daily accrualThe amount for a period is scaled by the share of trading days on which the share closed at or above the coupon accrual price.30,000 × 4% × 90 ÷ 120 days = 900.Coupon accrual price, calculation period, accrued days

Documents call every one of these a potential distribution amount. On an early call the investor receives the nominal amount plus whatever has accrued up to the call date, and nothing after it.

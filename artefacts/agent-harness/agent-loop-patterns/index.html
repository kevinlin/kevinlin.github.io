00The shape of the field

A coding agent on its own is a single forward pass: it reasons, edits, runs a tool, reads the result, and repeats until it decides it's done. Loop engineering is the craft of wrapping that raw capability in external structure — state, verification, budgets, and gates — so it produces trustworthy output instead of plausible-looking output. The patterns below are different answers to the same four questions: how do you iterate, how do you verify, how do you plan, and how many agents do you run? Most real systems combine several.

Where loop-orchestrator sits today

Your system is a guarded Maker–Checker loop with a deterministic verifier wedged in the middle. That puts it in the sweet spot the wider field has converged on: an LLM reviewer for judgment-heavy failures, plus a hard deterministic gate so nothing half-done ships. The git worktree is your blast-radius boundary; the budget and identical-failure breaker are your runaway-stop.

Maker (cheap model, edits in worktree) → protected-path check → Verifier (your shell command, exit 0 = pass) → Checker (strong model, JSON verdict) → guard (budget / breaker)

Your V4 direction — a Planner before the Maker and an LLM Judge replacing the binary verdict == "PASS" gate with a richer PASS | CONTINUE | REVISE_PLAN | REVISE_TASK | ESCALATE | HALT decision set — is exactly the move from "workflow" toward "agent" that Anthropic and others describe. 03What to take from this for your loop

Reading the field back onto loop-orchestrator and V4.

You already have the part everyone learns the hard way

The dominant 2026 lesson — "a loop without verification is just automation" — is that an LLM reviewer alone is too soft a gate, and you need a deterministic hard stop underneath it. Your Verifier + Checker pairing is precisely that two-tier design. Most Ralph-style setups bolt this on after getting burned.

V4's Judge is the field's frontier, not an over-engineering risk

The binary PASS gate can't tell "impossible" from "not done yet" from "oscillating but progressing." A semantic LLM-as-Judge with an escalation verdict is the recognised fix. Keep your safeguards: strict JSON schema, fall back to the binary gate on parse failure, and a minimum-iterations floor before ESCALATE.

Borrow specs, resist multi-agent (for now)

Spec-driven development would upgrade your MAKER.md/TASK.md anchor into durable acceptance criteria — cheap, high-leverage, and it feeds the Planner directly. Multi-maker parallelism is tempting but Huntley's "non-deterministic microservices" warning is real; your monolithic single-run design is a feature. Your worktrees already give you the isolation primitive if you ever fan out.

Protect the tests — you already do

Every test-driven loop eventually meets a model that edits the test to make it pass. Your protected_paths forcing a FAIL on diffs touching tests/ is the standard defence against reward-hacking the verifier. Keep it.
